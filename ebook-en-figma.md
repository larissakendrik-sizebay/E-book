## Page 3 — Presentation

### A whole year of opportunities starts with planning.

In fashion, being in the right moment makes all the difference. A trade show can anticipate trends, a fashion week can inspire a collection and a commercial date can represent a great sales opportunity.

But to make the most of every market movement, you need to prepare in advance.

With that in mind, we have gathered in this calendar the main dates that move the fashion industry around the world in 2027.

This material was designed to help you make more informed decisions: plan purchases, organize inventory, anticipate campaigns and get your store ready for the most important moments of the year.

Share it with your team and keep a whole year of opportunities on your radar.

The first knits and outerwear go live late in the month, right next to summer clearance. It's the moment to keep a full-price new-season story running while the sales do their job.

### E-commerce tip

Use summer sales to clear stock, not to train shoppers to wait. Segment markdown emails by purchase history, and be clear about which sizes are left and how they fit. Clearance leaves stock in fringe sizes, and shoppers who settle for the size they can find become August returns.

## Page 29 — Quarterly planning

### Your Quarter-Ahead Checklist

Run this checklist about eight weeks before each quarter starts. The first block repeats every quarter; the second covers what's specific to that quarter's peaks.

| Quarter · Run it by | Peaks to prepare | Quarter-specific checks |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 · Early Nov | Winter sales, Valentine's Day, early Easter (Mar 28), UK Mothering Sunday | Clearance edits by size availability; gift guides that work when the buyer isn't the wearer; spring imagery shot by mid-February |
| Q2 · Early Feb | Mother's Day (four dates), Memorial Day, Father's Day, Prime Day, summer sales | Swimwear fit content; one Mother's Day engine with per-market deadlines; a competing offer ready for Prime Day |
| Q3 · Early May | Summer clearance, back to school, Labor Day, fall launch | Kidswear size guidance by age and height; fall outerwear and boot fit notes; SS28 runway content templates |
| Q4 · Early Aug | Prime Big Deal Days, Singles' Day, Black Friday to Cyber Monday, Christmas, Boxing Day | Stock depth in core sizes on hero products; site and fit tools stress-tested by mid-October; extended holiday return windows communicated clearly |

## Pages 30–31 — Quarterly checklist

### Is Your Business Ready for the Next Quarter?

Use this checklist to review the key points of your operation's planning.

**1st quarter — January to March**
- Analyze last year's results.
- Review inventory and plan winter sales.
- Map trends and references for new collections.
- Plan purchasing and restocking.
- Organize campaigns for Valentine's Day, Presidents' Day and Easter.

**2nd quarter — April to June**
- Evaluate opportunities for special launches.
- Review product and size performance.
- Plan campaigns for Mother's Day, Father's Day and Memorial Day.
- Plan purchasing for Fall/Winter.
- Prepare the launch and promotion of Spring/Summer collections.

**3rd quarter — July to September**
- Plan sales and inventory renewal.
- Prepare Fall/Winter launches.
- Update storefronts and store content.
- Plan campaigns for Back to School and Labor Day.
- Start planning for Black Friday.

**4th quarter — October to December**
- Review inventory and size availability.
- Organize campaigns for Black Friday, Cyber Week and Christmas.
- Prepare your operation for a higher volume of orders.
- Set holiday shipping cutoffs and return windows.
- Analyze the year's results and plan for 2028.

**Remember:** Planning isn't something you do just once. Track your results, revise your strategies and adapt the calendar to your business's needs.

## Page 32 — Your next step

### The Dates Bring the Traffic. Confidence Closes the Sale.

Every peak on this calendar brings shoppers who behave differently from your regulars: gift buyers guessing someone else's size, first-time visitors who've never worn your brand, and customers stepping into categories they buy once a year. Before they buy, they all ask the same question: will this fit me? When the answer is a guess, you see it as hesitation at checkout, abandoned carts, bracketed orders and a heavy returns month after every peak.

Sizebay gives shoppers a complete decision experience right on the product page. Size recommendation suggests the right size based on the shopper's body and on how each piece is actually cut, and virtual try-on lets them see how it will look before they add it to the cart. Together, they turn calendar traffic into confident purchases, so the sales your campaigns generate are sales you keep.

Plan the experience alongside the promotions, and 2027 will reward both.
